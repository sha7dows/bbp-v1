Behavior-driven Hypergraph Modeling for Accurate
Cyberbullying Detection in Social Media
Xiaoyu Sean Lu 1, Zihao Ding 1, Xugong Qin 1, Di Wu 1,*, Jia Duan 1
1Nanjing University of Science and Technology
Abstract—With the widespread adoption of social media and
the growing complexity of online interactions, cyberbullying
has increasingly exhibited characteristics such as group involve-
ment, concealment, and coordinated behaviors. Most existing
approaches model interactions by using linear sequences or
binary graphs, which primarily capture simple local relationships
but struggle to mimic multi-user coordinated attacks effectively.
Moreover, these methods fail to model the relationship among
group behaviors that naturally emerge during the evolution
of cyberbullying. To address these challenges, we propose a
Behavior-driven Hypergraph Model for Cyberbullying Detection
(BeHM), which parses cyberbullying behaviors within media
sessions at two complementary levels: coordinated interactions
at the micro-level, and their relations at the macro-level. Then,
BeHM adopts a three-stage framework to capture the features at
the micro and macro levels. Experiments on two public session-
based social media datasets demonstrate that BeHM consistently
outperforms baselines, achieving an F1-score with improvements
of up to 1.3% on Instagram and 2.8% on Vine, and validating its
effectiveness in modeling complex group attacks and relationships
among group behaviors.
Index Terms—cyberbullying detection, social media analysis,
hypergraphs
I. INTRODUCTION
Defined as repeated aggressive behaviors carried out
through digital devices against vulnerable targets, cyberbul-
lying has become a global issue with the rapid growth of
social media [1]. Approximately half of young people have
experienced some forms of cyberbullying, and 10-20% faced
it regularly [2]. Victims often suffer from depression, anxiety,
and decreased self-esteem; in severe cases, they may even ex-
hibit self-harm or suicidal tendencies [3]. Therefore, effective
cyberbullying detection is critically important.
Recent studies have leveraged Graph Neural Networks
(GNNs) [4] to model media sessions through reply-to or user
interaction graphs [5], [6]. However, these pairwise graph-
based methods primarily capture individual user behaviors
(e.g., user A attacks user B), but struggle to model the coor-
dinated group behavior [7], [8], which manifests as collective
encirclement cyberbullying behaviors formed through interac-
tions among multiple users, as shown in Fig. 1. Moreover,
existing methods treat a group behavior as an isolated behavior
[9], [10], which neglects the relationship among different
group behaviors. E.g., a few groups attack the same victim,

As an extension of traditional graphs, hypergraphs leverage
their unique hyperedges to connect multiple nodes simultane-
ously and thus offer a more natural modeling framework for
describing coordinated behaviors [11]. These structures have
been widely applied in areas such as fake news detection
and hate speech detection. However, most existing studies
[12], [13] primarily model macroscopic relationships among
sessions rather than providing an in-depth analysis of the
complex interactions within each session.
To address these challenges, we propose a Behavior-driven
Hypergraph Model for Cyberbullying Detection (BeHM),
which captures cyberbullying through two complementary
levels. At the micro-level, we model and connect related
multi-user coordinated interactions with a hyperedge that
represents an individual group behavior, and at the macro-
level, an Interaction Behavior Graph (IBG) captures different
relations among group behaviors, and then, related relations
are formed as communities through a community discovery
method. Through a top-down fusion strategy, BeHM integrates
the features at these two complementary levels and develops
an effective identification of concealed and persistent cyberbul-
lying behaviors. Overall, the main contributions of this study

II. RELATEDWORK
A. Cyberbullying Detection
Over the past decade, the field of cyberbullying detection
has evolved from sequence modeling to graph modeling. Early
work models sessions as sequential data by using hierarchical
attention networks [16]. Cheng et al. [9] extend this with bidi-
rectional GRUs and hierarchical attentions to capture word-
level and comment-level contexts. Recent studies shift the
focus to user interactions by using Graph Neural Networks
(GNNs) [4]. Chen and Li [5] employ Graph Convolutional
Networks (GCNs) to capture inter-session and inter-post con-
nections, while Ge et al. [6] model the evolving interactions by
using a time graph. Building on this, Zhou et al. [17] introduce
TUHIN to model multidimensional social relationships via
heterogeneous graphs and temporal attention. However, tradi-
tional graphs are limited by binary connectivity and struggle to
capture group-based cyberbullying with multiple participants
(attackers, victims, bystanders). When GNNs model collective
attacks involving three or more users, their message-passing
mechanism suffers from information dilution. It creates low-
order representation bottlenecks, which weakens their ability
to handle complex group interactions.
B. Hypergraph Learning
Hypergraphs attract widespread attention due to their natural
ability to model complex high-order relationships. Hypergraph
Neural Networks (HGNNs) [18] explicitly capture high-order
relationships by constructing hyperedges among semantically
similar nodes based on distance metrics. The attention mecha-
nism for hypergraph introduced on this basis further enhances
the model’s power for expression. HyperGAT [19] employs a
two-level attention mechanism to capture high-order semantic
dependencies between words and sentences in text classifica-
tion tasks. Hypergraph neural networks are widely applied in
social media analysis. Jeong et al. [12] propose a framework
that constructs three types of hyperedges (user, time, and en-
tity) to capture group collaboration relationships during rumor
dissemination. Su and Yang [13] construct hypergraphs for
user attributes to represent non-textual high-order relationships
in the user participation for the news dissemination. Xu et
al. [20] propose a temporal hypergraph neural network for
information diffusion modeling, which captures both local
interactions and global diffusion patterns by jointly modeling
high-order interactions and their temporal dynamics. These
studies demonstrate the significant potential of hypergraphs
in capturing diverse interactions.

LetO={S 1, S2, . . . , SN }denote a dataset consisting of

the post and comments, and its associated ground truthY i.
Our objective is to model a sessionS i using a behavior-
driven hypergraph framework that captures coordinated group
behaviors at the micro level and the relationship among these
group behaviors at the macro level. By learning multi-level
representations that encode high-order interactions and their
structural associations, we aim to classify each session as
either bullying (Y i = 1) or non-bullying (Yi = 0).

consists of three core modules: 1) Hypergraph Construction,
which constructs a hypergraph for each session; 2) High-Order
Interaction Modeling, which treats hyperedges as coordinated
group behaviors and captures relationships among behaviors
from a macroscopic perspective; 3) Multi-dimensional Infor-
mation Fusion, which integrates information across multiple
levels to determine whether a session involves cyberbullying.

B. High-Order Interaction Modeling
After constructing the hypergraph, we apply a Hypergraph
Neural Network (HGNN) [18] to propagate information be-
tween content nodes and hyperedges. Through this process,
we obtain the node featuresX N and hyperedge featuresX E.
Hyperedge Association Network Construction.Traditional
hypergraph modeling typically treats hyperedges merely as
intermediate structures for message passing among nodes,
rather than as independent semantic entities. As a result, such
approaches fail to explicitly model the interactions and de-
pendencies among different behaviors [24]. In cyberbullying,
coordinated group behaviors are often closely related and mu-
tually reinforcing within a session. To explicitly model these
behaviors relationship, we propose the Interaction Behavior
Graph (IBG) denoted asG IB ={E H , EG}, which reinterprets
each hyperedgeE i ∈E H as a group behavior node. The edges
eij ∈E G model the interaction strength between different
behaviors, enabling the model to explicitly reason about how
group behaviors relate to each other within a session.

behaviors that share participants tend to exhibit strong asso-

between two hyperedgesE i andE j using Jaccard similarity,
which serves as the association weightW ij:
Wij = |Vi ∩V j| |Vi ∪V j| ,(5)
whereV i andV j denote the sets of content nodes participating
in hyperedgesE i andE j, respectively.
Collaborative Pattern Feature.We use the hyperedge repre-
sentationsX E learned by the HGNN as initial node features
of the IBG and apply a Graph Attention Network (GAT)
to propagate information among behavior nodes to obtain
updated representations. To identify groups of closely related
interaction behaviors in the IBG, we apply the Louvain com-
munity detection algorithm [25], which partitions the graph
into interaction behavior communities with dense connectivity
and high participant overlap. We then perform mean pooling
within each community to obtain community-level represen-
tationsC comm. These representations capture community-level
collaborative patterns.
C. Multi-dimensional Information Fusion
In this module, we employ a multi-dimensional attention
fusion mechanism to integrate information across nodes, hy-
peredges, and communities. Specifically, we first obtain the
global collaborative patternC G through attention pooling over
the community-level featuresC comm. We then concatenate
node and hyperedge features to formX L ={X N ;X E}.
Conditioned onC G, an attention mechanism highlights local
content nodes and group behaviors that are most consistent
with the global collaborative pattern. Finally, the attended local
features are fused withC G through a residual connection to
produce the final session representationC final as follows:

V. EXPERIMENTS
A. Dataset
We conduct experiments on two public datasets, Instagram
[14] and Vine [15]. These datasets are widely recognized and
used in media-session-based cyberbullying studies.
B. Experimental Setup
1) Evaluation Metrics:We use five standard evaluation
metrics for comparisons among cyberbullying detection mod-
els [5], [6], [9]: Accuracy, F1, Precision, Recall, and AUC.
2) Baseline Methods:To comprehensively evaluate the
effectiveness of our approach, we compare it against represen-
tative baselines that model cyberbullying detection from three
mainstream modeling paradigms: (1) sequence-based session
modeling methods: HANCD [9] and LS-CB [27], (2) graph-
based session modeling methods: GNN [4], HENIN [5] and
TGBully [6], (3) hypergraph-based session modeling methods:
HGNN [18], HGFND [12] and HA-GCEN [23].
3) Parameter Settings:The semantic-temporal coordinated
behavior hyperedge construction thresholdsθare set to0.65
and0.6for the Instagram and Vine datasets, respectively.
During the hypergraph construction process, we set semantic
sensitivityτto0.1, semantic compensationηto0.55, and
temporal weightβto0.5. We train the model for 40 epochs
with a learning rate of10 −4 and dropout of0.2. We split the
dataset into 80% for training and 20% for testing. We report
results averaged over five runs.

VI. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we investigate the collective behavioral pat-
terns underlying cyberbullying and the limitations of existing
sequential and pairwise graph methods in modeling high-
order interactions. To address these limitations, we propose
BeHM, a behavior-driven hypergraph framework. BeHM cap-
tures coordinated attack behaviors at the micro level and
their relationship at the macro level through hyperedge con-
struction, interaction behavior graph modeling, and multi-
dimensional information fusion. Experimental results on two
public datasets show that BeHM consistently outperforms ex-
isting methods. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of
high-order relational modeling and layered multi-dimensional
fusion for session-level cyberbullying detection. Future work
can extend this framework to finer-grained behavior analysis,
cross-session relation modeling, and multimodal integration.
